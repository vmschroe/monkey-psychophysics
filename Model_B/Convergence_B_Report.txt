\title{Model B: Posterior Convergence Study}
\author{Vera Schroeder}
\date{September 30, 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We simulated data from Model B with known hyperparameters and refit the model
while growing the dataset in two directions: more sessions, and more trials per
session. At the size of the real dataset (42 sessions of about 308 trials),
per-session PSE and JND estimates are accurate, with 95\% HDI coverage close to
nominal. The group biases $\mu_{b_0}$, the left-hand slopes $\mu_{b_1}$ and the
between-session spread of the bias $\sigma_{b_0}$ are also recovered well.
Three results limit what can be concluded from the real fit.
(i)~The right-hand slopes (true $\mu_{b_1}=6$) are weakly constrained, and their
HDIs reach the prior's upper bound of 8.
(ii)~The between-session standard deviations of the slope and of both lapse
rates are not identified: their true values are close to zero, yet the posterior
means stay at 0.2--0.8 even with 80 sessions.
(iii)~For the left-hand groups, the lower lapse rate $\gamma_l$ is overestimated
(about 0.014--0.024 against a true 0.0055), and more trials per session do not
remove the bias.
\end{abstract}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Design}

The study is implemented in \texttt{Model\_B/Convergence\_B.py}; results are
stored in \texttt{Model\_B/Results\_Convergence\_B.pkl}.

\paragraph{True values.}
The hyperparameters are fixed at the values in \texttt{ReadyData\_Synth\_B.pkl}
(Table~\ref{tab:truth}). The four groups are the combinations of hand (left,
right) and condition (unimanual, bimanual). Each synthetic replicate draws new
session-level parameters from these hyperparameters:
\begin{align*}
b_{0,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{b_0,g},\, \sigma_{b_0,g}), &
b_{1,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{b_1,g},\, \sigma_{b_1,g}), \\
\gamma_{h,gs} &= 0.25\,\operatorname{logit}^{-1}\!\big(\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\gamma_h,g},\, \sigma_{\gamma_h,g})\big), &
\gamma_{l,gs} &= 0.25\,\operatorname{logit}^{-1}\!\big(\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\gamma_l,g},\, \sigma_{\gamma_l,g})\big),
\end{align*}
and responses are Bernoulli with probability
$\psi = \gamma_h + (1-\gamma_h-\gamma_l)\,\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(b_0 + b_1 x)$.
Stimuli are resampled from each group's stimuli in the real design, and groups
cycle within a session so every session is balanced.

\paragraph{Relevant priors of Model B} (from \texttt{Build\_Model\_B.py}):
$\mu_{b_0}\sim\mathcal{U}[-12,12]$, $\mu_{b_1}\sim\mathcal{U}[0,8]$,
$\sigma_{b_0}\sim\text{Exp}(\text{mean }12)$, $\sigma_{b_1}\sim\text{Exp}(\text{mean }4)$,
$\mu_{\gamma}\sim\mathcal{N}(-2.5,1)$, $\sigma_{\gamma}\sim\text{HalfNormal}(1.25)$.

\paragraph{Sweeps.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sessions sweep:} 5, 10, 20, 40, 80 sessions, each with 308 trials
        (the real average). This mainly tests recovery of the hyperparameters.
  \item \textbf{Trials sweep:} 42 sessions (the real number), each with 20, 40,
        80, 160, 320 trials. This mainly tests recovery of the session-level
        parameters. Here the hyperparameter posteriors can at best converge to
        the mean and sd of the 42 sessions actually drawn, not to the population
        values.
\end{itemize}
Within a replicate the datasets are nested (the first $n$ sessions, or the first
$t$ trials of every session), so differences between sizes come from the added
data and not from a new random draw. Each sweep was run with 2 independent
replicates. Each fit used 4 chains of 1000 tuning and 1000 retained draws. The
whole run took about 2.5 hours.

\paragraph{The real data} (42 sessions $\times$ $\approx$308 trials) corresponds
to the 40-session point of the sessions sweep and the 320-trial point of the
trials sweep. These are the most relevant points for interpreting the real fit.

\paragraph{Bias $\mu_{b_0}$.}
Recovered well in all groups. From 20 sessions onward the HDIs are narrow and
contain the true value. At 40 sessions the posterior means are within 0.22 of the
truth, with HDI widths of about 0.7--0.8.

\paragraph{Slope $\mu_{b_1}$.}
For the left-hand groups (true value 3), the posterior converges to the truth
(at 80 sessions: 3.03 and 3.28, HDI width about 0.5--0.6). For the right-hand
groups (true value 6), the HDIs remain wide, about 2 units at 40 sessions, and
reach the prior's upper bound of 8 up to 40 sessions. At 40 sessions the
right\_uni posterior mean is 6.9. Steep psychometric functions are harder to pin
down with a fixed set of stimulus levels, so in the real data the right-hand
slopes, and the quantities derived from them, are the least certain
population-level results. The upper bound of 8 on $\mu_{b_1}$ truncates these
posteriors, and it would bind if a real group's slope were near or above it.

\paragraph{Lapse-rate means $\mu_{\gamma_h}$ and $\mu_{\gamma_l}$.}
$\mu_{\gamma_h}$ is recovered, with the intervals narrowing slowly as data grows.
$\mu_{\gamma_l}$ is overestimated for the left-hand groups, whose true values
($-3.8$, $-3.9$) lie well below the prior centre of $-2.5$: at 40 sessions
the posterior means are $-3.30$ and $-3.57$, and only at 80 sessions does left\_uni
reach $-3.69$. More trials per session do not fix this: at 320 trials
left\_uni is still at $-2.62$ (true $-3.8$). The right-hand groups show no
consistent direction of error. The HDIs contain the true value in nearly every
fit, so the error is inside the stated uncertainty. However, the posterior
means are systematically too high for the left-hand groups.

$\sigma_{b_0}$ is recovered well from 20 sessions on (Table~\ref{tab:sds80}).
The other three sds, $\sigma_{b_1}$, $\sigma_{\gamma_h}$ and $\sigma_{\gamma_l}$,
have true values near zero, meaning the simulated sessions barely differ in slope
or lapse rate. Their posteriors decline slowly as sessions are added, but even at
80 sessions the posterior means are 0.16--0.65, one to two orders of magnitude
above the truth. All their HDIs extend down to 0, so the true value is always
covered. This coverage only shows that zero cannot be ruled out; it does not
mean the sds are estimated. At the real data size these sds are not identified,
and their posteriors largely reflect the priors ($\text{Exp}$ with mean 4 for
$\sigma_{b_1}$, $\text{HalfNormal}(1.25)$ for the lapse sds). They should not be
interpreted from the real fit.

A consequence carries over to the session level: because the model believes
sessions vary more than they do, it shrinks session estimates less toward the
group mean than it could, and their HDIs are wider than necessary (Section~\ref{sec:session}).

\paragraph{PSE, JND, $b_0$, $b_1$.}
Error and HDI width fall steadily as trials per session grow
(Figure~\ref{fig:session_trials}); at 320 trials the RMSE of the PSE is
0.05--0.14 and of the JND 0.01--0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:session320}). The largest
drop is between 40 and 80 trials; below 80 trials the JND in particular is
poorly determined (RMSE up to 0.5 at 20 trials). Adding sessions at a fixed 308
trials (Figure~\ref{fig:session_sessions}) also improves the session estimates,
because better-estimated hyperparameters give better shrinkage.

\paragraph{Coverage.}
The coverage of $b_1$, JND and both lapse rates is essentially 1.0: the HDIs are
wider than needed, a consequence of the overestimated between-session sds. PSE
coverage at 320 trials is slightly below nominal (0.89--0.94). With 84 sessions
per group, the standard error of a coverage estimate near 0.95 is about 0.024,
so 0.89 is about 2.5 standard errors low. This mild undercoverage of the PSE is
worth keeping in mind but is not large. At 40 sessions, one replicate's upward
error in $\mu_{b_1}$ for left\_bi (posterior mean 3.45) lowers the coverage of
$b_1$ and JND in that group to 0.86 and 0.88, which shows that errors at the
population level carry through to every session of the group.

\paragraph{Lower lapse rate $\gamma_l$ for left\_uni.}
Its RMSE stays at about 0.02 regardless of the number of trials
(Figure~\ref{fig:session_trials}). The true session values are about 0.0055;
the posterior means average 0.024 at 320 trials, roughly four times too high.
This is the $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ bias from Section~3 passed down to every session
through shrinkage. In absolute terms the error is small (a lapse rate of about
2\% instead of 0.5\%), but relative to the true value it is large.

\section{Conclusions for the real data}

At the size of the real dataset (42 sessions of about 308 trials):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reliable:} per-session PSE and JND; group means $\mu_{b_0}$;
        $\mu_{b_1}$ for the left-hand groups; the bias spread $\sigma_{b_0}$;
        the upper lapse rate $\mu_{\gamma_h}$.
  \item \textbf{Weakly constrained:} $\mu_{b_1}$ for the right-hand groups, whose
        HDIs are about 2 units wide and reach the prior bound of 8.
  \item \textbf{Biased:} $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ and the per-session $\gamma_l$ for the
        left-hand groups, which are estimated too high, apparently pulled
        toward the prior centre of $-2.5$.
  \item \textbf{Not identified:} $\sigma_{b_1}$, $\sigma_{\gamma_h}$,
        $\sigma_{\gamma_l}$ when their true values are near zero. Posteriors for
        these reflect the priors and should not be interpreted.
  \item Session-level HDIs are, if anything, conservative (coverage near or
        above 95\%), with a mild undercoverage for the PSE.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Possible follow-ups.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Raise the upper bound of the $\mu_{b_1}$ prior (e.g.\ to 12) and check
        whether the right-hand slopes are still truncated.
  \item Test whether the $\gamma_l$ bias comes from the $\mathcal{N}(-2.5,1)$
        prior on $\mu_\gamma$, by refitting with a wider or lower-centred prior.
  \item Consider tighter priors on $\sigma_{b_1}$ and the lapse-rate sds, which
        would shrink the session estimates more and narrow their HDIs, if
        small between-session variation is plausible for the real animals.
\end{enumerate}
