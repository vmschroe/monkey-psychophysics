\section{Model Structure}
\subsection{Data and indexing}
The data are $N$ binary trials. Trial $i$ has
\begin{itemize}
  \item a response $y_i \in \{0,1\}$, the monkey's categorization of the stimulus as ``low'' (0) or ``high'' (1);
  \item a standardized stimulus amplitude $x_i$. The raw amplitudes are centered and scaled by the mean and standard deviation of the distinct amplitude levels. The covariate row is
        $(1, x_i)$;
  \item a group $g[i] \in \{1,\dots,4\}$: the combination of
        hand and distractor condition,
        \[
          g \in \{\text{left\_uni},\ \text{left\_bi},\ \text{right\_uni},\ \text{right\_bi}\},
        \]
        where ``uni'' is unimanual (no distractor) and ``bi'' is bimanual
        (distractor present);
  \item a session $s[i] \in \{1,\dots,S\}$; the real data have
        $S = 42$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Likelihood}


Each response is Bernoulli,
\[
  y_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\psi_i), \qquad
  \psi_i = \psi\big(x_i;\ \theta_{g[i],s[i]}\big),
\]
with the psychometric function
\[
  \psi(x;\theta) = \gamma_h + (1 - \gamma_h - \gamma_l)\,\invlogit(\beta_0 + \beta_1 x),
  \qquad \theta = (\beta_0, \beta_1, \gamma_h, \gamma_l).
\]
Here $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ are parameters of the sigmoid, and $\gamma_h,\gamma_l$
set the asymptotes:
\[
  \lim_{x\to-\infty}\psi = \gamma_h, \qquad \lim_{x\to+\infty}\psi = 1-\gamma_l .
\]
So $\gamma_h$ is the probability of responding 1 (``high'') at very low amplitudes, and
$\gamma_l$ the probability of responding 0 (``low'') at very high amplitudes. In the code
$\psi_i$ is clipped to $[10^{-8},\,1-10^{-8}]$ before entering the Bernoulli
likelihood, for numerical stability.

\subsection{Session-level}

For each group $g$ and session $s$, the four parameters are drawn independently
from group-specific distributions:
\begin{align*}
  \beta_{0,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{\beta_0,g},\ \sigma_{\beta_0,g}\big), \\
  \beta_{1,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{\beta_1,g},\ \sigma_{\beta_1,g}\big), \\
  \gamma_{h,gs} &= 0.25\, w_{h,gs}, \qquad
      w_{h,gs} \sim \text{LogitNormal}\big(\mu_{\gamma_h,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_h,g}\big), \\
  \gamma_{l,gs} &= 0.25\, w_{l,gs}, \qquad
      w_{l,gs} \sim \text{LogitNormal}\big(\mu_{\gamma_l,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_l,g}\big).
\end{align*}
The LogitNormal keeps $w \in (0,1)$, so each lapse parameter $\gamma$ lies in
$(0, 0.25)$. That range keeps the curve increasing and makes the PSE and JND
below well defined.

Groups are independent of each other (no pooling across groups).

\subsection{Group-level Hyperpriors}

Each of the four groups has its own 8 hyperparameters, with the same priors in
every group:
\begin{align*}
  \mu_{\beta_0,g} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\ 2), &
  \sigma_{\beta_0,g} &\sim \text{HalfNormal}(2), \\
  \mu_{\beta_1,g} &\sim \text{LogNormal}(\log 4,\ 0.5), &
  \sigma_{\beta_1,g} &\sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda = 1) \quad (\text{mean } 1), \\
  \mu_{\gamma_h,g},\ \mu_{\gamma_l,g} &\sim \mathcal{N}(-3,\ 1.5), &
  \sigma_{\gamma_h,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_l,g} &\sim \text{HalfNormal}(0.5).
\end{align*}
All hyperparameters are independent. The priors were chosen from the stimulus
design and general knowledge of the task, and checked by prior predictive
simulation (\code{Check\_Prior\_B.py}), not from fits to the real data:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mu_{\beta_0}$: the stimulus amplitudes (6 to 50) are symmetric around 28,
        the category boundary, which standardizes to $x = 0$. This prior puts a
        group's typical PSE between amplitudes 13 and 43 with 90\% probability,
        and outside the stimulus range only 3\% of the time.
  \item $\sigma_{\beta_0}$: weakly informative, bounded by the design. The implied
        day-to-day sd of the PSE has median about 5 amplitude units and is below
        about 20 with 95\% probability, small next to the stimulus range.
  \item $\mu_{\beta_1}$: positive with no upper bound; median 4, 95\% in
        $[1.5,\ 10.6]$.
  \item $\sigma_{\beta_1}$: mean 1, so that sessions vary around the group slope
        without the session slopes turning negative (2\% prior probability).
  \item $\mu_\gamma$ (logit scale): the typical lapse rate
        $0.25\,\invlogit(\mu_\gamma)$ has median 0.012 and 95\% range
        $[0.0007,\ 0.12]$; trained animals lapse on a few percent of trials and
        rarely more than about 10\%.
  \item $\sigma_\gamma$ (logit scale): median 0.34. For a group whose typical
        lapse rate is 0.05, this puts about 80\% of sessions between 0.03 and 0.07.
\end{itemize}

An earlier version of the model used
$\mu_{\beta_0} \sim \mathcal{U}[-12,\ 12]$,
$\sigma_{\beta_0} \sim \text{Exponential}(\text{mean } 12)$,
$\mu_{\beta_1} \sim \mathcal{U}[0,\ 8]$,
$\sigma_{\beta_1} \sim \text{Exponential}(\text{mean } 4)$,
$\mu_\gamma \sim \mathcal{N}(-2.5,\ 1)$ and
$\sigma_\gamma \sim \text{HalfNormal}(1.25)$.
Its convergence study motivated the changes; Section~\ref{sec:prior_comparison}
compares the two versions.

\subsection{Derived Quantities: PSE and JND}

For each group and session the model records two summaries of the psychometric
curve.

\paragraph{Point of subjective equality (PSE):} the stimulus at which a response of ``high'' and ``low'' are equally likely ($\psi = 1/2$). Solving
$\psi(x;\theta) = \tfrac12$ for x gives
\[
  \text{PSE} = \frac{1}{\beta_1}\left[-\beta_0 + \log\frac{1-2\gamma_h}{1-2\gamma_l}\right].
\]

\paragraph{Just noticeable difference (JND):} the amount the stimulus must be changed for the
difference to be noticeable half of the time, a measure of sensitivity. This is computed as half the stimulus distance between the points where $\psi = 0.25$ and $\psi = 0.75$,
\[
  \text{JND} = \frac{x_{.75} - x_{.25}}{2}
  = \frac{1}{2\beta_1}\,\log\frac{(3-4\gamma_h)(3-4\gamma_l)}{(1-4\gamma_h)(1-4\gamma_l)} .
\]
Both are in units of the standardized stimulus. When
$\gamma_h=\gamma_l=0$, they reduce to $\text{PSE} = - \frac{\beta_0}{\beta_1} $ and $\text{JND} =  \frac{\log 3}{\beta_1}$.

\subsection{Implementation}

To aid fit, as in the convention of typical logistic regression, the raw amplitudes $x_i$ are centered and scaled by the mean and standard deviation of the distinct amplitude levels. To aid convergence of HMC NUTS, all distributions are written as deterministic transforms of a standard normal or uniform random variable. These non-centered parameterizations mean the sampler only works with uniform or standard normal variables.

\section{Synthetic Data Generation}

I fixed ``hyperparameters'' and simulated 10 total dataset conditions in two sweeps, and fit the model to each dataset. Each sweep was performed twice with new data. The true hyperparameters are the dashed lines in the recovery plots below; in particular, the true group slopes are $\mu_{\beta_1} = 3$ for the left hand and 6 for the right hand.

The first sweep held trials-per-session constant and varied number of sessions (5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 sessions, each with 308 trials). This provides info on convergence of global hyperparameters.

The second held session count constant, varying trials-per-session (42 sessions, each with 20, 40, 80, 160 or 320 trials). This provides info on convergence of session-level parameters.

The real data has 42 sessions with about 308 trials per session, so the 40-session and 320-trial fits are the most representative.

Both versions of the model (current and earlier priors) were fit to exactly the same synthetic datasets, so any difference between their results comes from the priors alone. The results below are for the current priors; Section~\ref{sec:prior_comparison} compares the two.

\paragraph{Bias $\mu_{\beta_0}$:}
Recovered well in all groups. At 40 sessions the posterior means (averaged over
replicates) are within 0.22 of the truth, with HDI widths of 0.56--0.85.

\paragraph{Slope $\mu_{\beta_1}$:}
For the left-hand groups (true value $\mu_{\beta_1} = 3$), the posterior converges
to the truth (at 80 sessions: 2.98 [2.76, 3.23] and 3.24 [2.93, 3.56]). For the
right-hand groups (true value 6) the posterior is much less precise: at 40
sessions right\_uni is 6.89 [5.60, 8.22] and right\_bi 6.11 [5.23, 7.01], and at
80 sessions 6.32 [5.50, 7.11] and 6.13 [5.45, 6.79]. The posteriors are no
longer truncated at 8, as they were under the earlier bounded prior; the HDI of
right\_uni now reaches up to about 8.9 at 40 sessions in one replicate.

The main cause is the stimulus design rather than the prior. At a slope of 6
the rise from $\psi = 0.25$ to $\psi = 0.75$ spans $2\log 3/6 \approx 0.37$
standardized units, less than the spacing between stimulus levels (about 0.41),
so only the two levels nearest the PSE carry information about the slope; at the
other levels the curve is already at its floor or ceiling. The data can therefore
rule out shallower slopes but not steeper ones, so the right-hand posteriors are
wide and, in the sessions sweep, lean slightly above the truth. Only stimulus levels spaced more closely
around the PSE would make steep slopes better identified.

\paragraph{Lapse-rate means $\mu_{\gamma_h}$ and $\mu_{\gamma_l}$:}
The lapse rates are only identifiable at stimulus levels where the sigmoid has
reached its floor or ceiling, and lapses are rare there (about 2 expected lapses
of $\gamma_l$ per stimulus level for the left-hand groups at the real data size).

$\mu_{\gamma_h}$ is recovered for left\_uni and both right-hand groups (HDI widths
0.7--1.1 at 40 sessions). For left\_bi, whose sigmoid never reaches its floor
within the stimulus range, the posterior is wide and in the trials sweep sits
below the truth: $-2.79$ [$-4.79$, $-1.23$] at 320 trials (true $-1.9$).

$\mu_{\gamma_l}$ for the left-hand groups is now recovered without bias in the
sessions sweep: at 40 sessions $-3.86$ for both (true $-3.8$ and $-3.9$),
although the HDIs are wide (2.7--3.9). In the trials sweep left\_uni is still
too high, $-2.73$ [$-4.55$, $-1.38$] at 320 trials. The left\_uni sigmoid only
reaches 0.97 at the highest stimulus, so a shortfall at the top can be explained
by $\gamma_l$ or by the slope and bias, and with so few lapses the estimate
depends strongly on the particular dataset. For right\_uni the HDI at 80 sessions
excludes the true value in both replicates ($-2.36$ [$-2.77$, $-1.96$], true
$-2.8$). The estimate was just as high under the earlier priors ($-2.37$, with
the HDI excluding the truth in one of the two replicates), so it is not caused by
the prior, and its cause has not been identified.

\paragraph{PSE, JND, $\beta_0$, $\beta_1$.}
Error and HDI width fall steadily as trials per session grow
(Figure~\ref{fig:session_trials}); at 320 trials the RMSE of the PSE is
0.05--0.14 and of the JND 0.01--0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:session320}). The largest
drop is between 40 and 80 trials; below 80 trials the JND in particular is
poorly determined for some groups (RMSE 0.40 and 0.34 for left\_uni at 20 and
40 trials, 0.19 for right\_uni), falling to 0.05 or less at 80 trials. Adding sessions at a fixed 308
trials (Figure~\ref{fig:session_sessions}) also improves the session estimates,
because better-estimated hyperparameters give better shrinkage.

\paragraph{Coverage.}
The coverage of $\beta_1$ and JND is essentially 1.0: the HDIs are wider than
needed, because the between-session sd of the slope is overestimated. PSE
coverage at 320 trials is slightly below nominal (0.90--0.94). With 84 sessions
per group, the standard error of a coverage estimate near 0.95 is about 0.024,
so 0.90 is about 2 standard errors low: a mild undercoverage worth keeping in
mind. Errors at the population level carry through to every session of a group:
at 40 sessions, one replicate's upward error in $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for left\_bi
(3.73 against a true 3) lowers the coverage of $\beta_1$ and JND in that group
to 0.72 and 0.78 in that replicate (0.86 and 0.89 over both replicates).

\paragraph{Lapse rates $\gamma_h$ and $\gamma_l$.}
The tighter prior on $\sigma_\gamma$ shrinks the session lapse rates more strongly
toward their group's value, so their HDIs are narrower than under the earlier
priors (mean width at 320 trials 0.058 for $\gamma_h$ and 0.028 for $\gamma_l$).
Coverage is mostly 1.0, but a miss at the group level now shows up in every
session of the group: at 160 trials, one replicate's right\_bi group $\gamma_l$
was estimated at 0.004 against a true 0.011, which lowers the overall $\gamma_l$
coverage at that size to 0.89.

For left\_uni, $\gamma_l$ remains too high in the trials sweep: at 320 trials
the posterior means average 0.021 against a true 0.0056 (RMSE 0.016). This is the
$\mu_{\gamma_l}$ error described above passed down to every session. In the
sessions sweep the same quantity is recovered well (RMSE 0.003 at 40 sessions).
For left\_bi, $\gamma_h$ is too low in the trials sweep (0.021 against 0.032 at
320 trials), the session-level counterpart of the $\mu_{\gamma_h}$ error.

In summary, the current priors
\begin{itemize}
  \item remove the sampling problems of the small (5-session) datasets;
  \item remove the truncation of the right-hand slopes at 8, so their HDIs now
        honestly reflect how little the design says about steep slopes;
  \item remove the upward bias of $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ for the left-hand groups in
        the sessions sweep; the bias remains for left\_uni in the trials sweep,
        where it comes from the curve not reaching its ceiling rather than from
        the prior;
  \item narrow the session-level lapse-rate HDIs, at the cost of occasional
        whole-group misses (Section~\ref{sec:session}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusions for the real data}

At the size of the real dataset (42 sessions of about 308 trials):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reliable:} per-session PSE and JND; group means $\mu_{\beta_0}$;
        $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for the left-hand groups; the bias spread $\sigma_{\beta_0}$;
        $\mu_{\gamma_h}$ except for left\_bi.
  \item \textbf{Weakly constrained:} $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for the right-hand groups,
        whose HDIs are about 1.8--2.6 units wide at 40 sessions.
  \item \textbf{Biased in some datasets:} lapse rates whose side of the curve
        does not reach its asymptote within the stimulus range: $\gamma_l$ for
        left\_uni (estimated too high) and $\gamma_h$ for left\_bi (too low).
  \item \textbf{Not identified:} $\sigma_{\beta_1}$, $\sigma_{\gamma_h}$ and
        $\sigma_{\gamma_l}$ when their true values are near zero. Their posteriors
        largely reflect the priors and should not be interpreted.
  \item Session-level HDIs are mostly conservative (coverage near or above 95\%),
        with a mild undercoverage for the PSE (0.90--0.94 at 320 trials).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Possible follow-ups.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Done (October 2026):} the real data were refit with the current
        priors; see the separate report ``Model B Fit to the Real Data''
        (\code{Fit\_B\_Report.txt}).
  \item Find out why $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ for right\_uni is overestimated at 80
        sessions in both replicates and under both sets of priors.
  \item Consider removing the session-level variation of the lapse rates
        (one $\gamma_h$ and $\gamma_l$ per group), since $\sigma_{\gamma_l}$ and
        $\sigma_{\gamma_h}$ are not identified; this would also simplify the model.
  \item For future experiments: stimulus levels spaced more closely around the
        PSE would identify steep slopes, and levels further out would let the
        curves reach their asymptotes and identify the lapse rates.
\end{enumerate}
