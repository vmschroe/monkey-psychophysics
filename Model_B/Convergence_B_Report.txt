\section{Model Structure}
\subsection{Data and indexing}
The data are $N$ binary trials. Trial $i$ has
\begin{itemize}
  \item a response $y_i \in \{0,1\}$, the monkey's categorization of the stimulus as ``low'' (0) or ``high'' (1);
  \item a standardized stimulus amplitude $x_i$. The raw amplitudes are centered and scaled by the mean and standard deviation of the distinct amplitude levels. The covariate row is
        $(1, x_i)$;
  \item a group $g[i] \in \{1,\dots,4\}$: the combination of
        hand and distractor condition,
        \[
          g \in \{\text{left\_uni},\ \text{left\_bi},\ \text{right\_uni},\ \text{right\_bi}\},
        \]
        where ``uni'' is unimanual (no distractor) and ``bi'' is bimanual
        (distractor present);
  \item a session $s[i] \in \{1,\dots,S\}$; the real data have
        $S = 42$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Likelihood}


Each response is Bernoulli,
\[
  y_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\psi_i), \qquad
  \psi_i = \psi\big(x_i;\ \theta_{g[i],s[i]}\big),
\]
with the psychometric function
\[
  \psi(x;\theta) = \gamma_h + (1 - \gamma_h - \gamma_l)\,\invlogit(\beta_0 + \beta_1 x),
  \qquad \theta = (\beta_0, \beta_1, \gamma_h, \gamma_l).
\]
Here $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ are parameters of the sigmoid, and $\gamma_h,\gamma_l$
set the asymptotes:
\[
  \lim_{x\to-\infty}\psi = \gamma_h, \qquad \lim_{x\to+\infty}\psi = 1-\gamma_l .
\]
So $\gamma_h$ is the probability of responding 1 (``high'') at very low amplitudes, and
$\gamma_l$ the probability of responding 0 (``low'') at very high amplitudes. In the code
$\psi_i$ is clipped to $[10^{-8},\,1-10^{-8}]$ before entering the Bernoulli
likelihood, for numerical stability.

\subsection{Session-level}

For each group $g$ and session $s$, the four parameters are drawn independently
from group-specific distributions:
\begin{align*}
  \beta_{0,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{\beta_0,g},\ \sigma_{\beta_0,g}\big), \\
  \beta_{1,gs} &\sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_{\beta_1,g},\ \sigma_{\beta_1,g}\big), \\
  \gamma_{h,gs} &= 0.25\, w_{h,gs}, \qquad
      w_{h,gs} \sim \text{LogitNormal}\big(\mu_{\gamma_h,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_h,g}\big), \\
  \gamma_{l,gs} &= 0.25\, w_{l,gs}, \qquad
      w_{l,gs} \sim \text{LogitNormal}\big(\mu_{\gamma_l,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_l,g}\big).
\end{align*}
The LogitNormal keeps $w \in (0,1)$, so each lapse parameter $\gamma$ lies in
$(0, 0.25)$. That range keeps the curve increasing and makes the PSE and JND
below well defined.

Groups are independent of each other (no pooling across groups).

\subsection{Group-level Hyperpriors}


Each of the four groups has its own 8 hyperparameters, with the same priors in
every group:
\begin{align*}
  \mu_{\beta_0,g} &\sim \mathcal{U}[-12,\ 12], &
  \sigma_{\beta_0,g} &\sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda = 1/12) \quad (\text{mean } 12), \\
  \mu_{\beta_1,g} &\sim \mathcal{U}[0,\ 8], &
  \sigma_{\beta_1,g} &\sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda = 1/4) \quad (\text{mean } 4), \\
  \mu_{\gamma_h,g},\ \mu_{\gamma_l,g} &\sim \mathcal{N}(-2.5,\ 1), &
  \sigma_{\gamma_h,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_l,g} &\sim \text{HalfNormal}(1.25).
\end{align*}
The prior on $\mu_{\beta_1}$ restricts the typical slope to be non-negative and at
most 8. The lapse means are on the logit scale; the prior center $-2.5$
corresponds to a typical lapse parameter of $0.25\,\invlogit(-2.5) \approx 0.02$.
All hyperparameters are independent.

These are the priors used for the simulations in this report. Since then, the
following priors in \code{Build\_Model\_B.py} have been changed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mu_{\beta_1,g} \sim \text{LogNormal}(\log 4,\ 0.5)$ (median 4, 95\% in
        $[1.5,\ 10.7]$, no upper bound);
  \item $\sigma_{\beta_1,g} \sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda = 1)$ (mean 1);
  \item $\mu_{\gamma_h,g},\ \mu_{\gamma_l,g} \sim \mathcal{N}(-3,\ 1.5)$, so the
        typical lapse rate $0.25\,\invlogit(\mu_\gamma)$ has median $0.012$ and 95\%
        range $[0.0007,\ 0.12]$. The wider spread weakens the pull of the prior
        when the data contain only a few lapses;
  \item $\sigma_{\gamma_h,g},\ \sigma_{\gamma_l,g} \sim \text{HalfNormal}(0.5)$
        (median 0.34). For a group whose typical lapse rate is 0.05, this puts
        about 80\% of sessions between 0.03 and 0.07.
\end{itemize}
The convergence results below have not been rerun with the new priors.

\subsection{Derived Quantities: PSE and JND}

For each group and session the model records two summaries of the psychometric
curve.

\paragraph{Point of subjective equality (PSE):} the stimulus at which a response of ``high'' and ``low'' are equally likely ($\psi = 1/2$). Solving
$\psi(x;\theta) = \tfrac12$ for x gives
\[
  \text{PSE} = \frac{1}{\beta_1}\left[-\beta_0 + \log\frac{1-2\gamma_h}{1-2\gamma_l}\right].
\]

\paragraph{Just noticeable difference (JND):} the amount the stimulus must be changed for the
difference to be noticeable half of the time, a measure of sensitivity. This is computed as half the stimulus distance between the points where $\psi = 0.25$ and $\psi = 0.75$,
\[
  \text{JND} = \frac{x_{.75} - x_{.25}}{2}
  = \frac{1}{2\beta_1}\,\log\frac{(3-4\gamma_h)(3-4\gamma_l)}{(1-4\gamma_h)(1-4\gamma_l)} .
\]
Both are in units of the standardized stimulus. When
$\gamma_h=\gamma_l=0$, they reduce to $\text{PSE} = - \frac{\beta_0}{\beta_1} $ and $\text{JND} =  \frac{\log 3}{\beta_1}$.

\subsection{Implementation}

To aid fit, as in the convention of typical logistic regression, the raw amplitudes $x_i$ are centered and scaled by the mean and standard deviation of the distinct amplitude levels. To aid convergence of HMC NUTS, all distributions are written as deterministic transforms of a standard normal or uniform random variable. These non-centered parameterizations mean the sampler only works with uniform or standard normal variables.

\section{Synthetic Data Generation}

I fixed ``hyperparameters'' and simulated 10 total dataset conditions in two sweeps, and fit the model to each dataset. Each sweep was performed twice with new data.

The first sweep held trials-per-session constant and varied number of sessions (5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 sessions, each with 308 trials). This provides info on convergence of global hyperparameters.

The second held session count constant, varying trials-per-session (42 sessions, each with 20, 40, 80, 160 or 320 trials). This provides info on convergence of session-level parameters.

The real data has 42 sessions with about 308 trials per session, so the 40-session and 320-trial fits are the most representative.

\paragraph{Bias $\mu_{\beta_0}$:}
Recovered well in all groups. From 20 sessions onward the HDIs are narrow and
contain the true value. At 40 sessions the posterior means are within 0.22 of the
truth, with HDI widths of about 0.7--0.8.

\paragraph{Slope $\mu_{\beta_1}$:}
For the left-hand groups (true value fixed $\mu_{\beta_1} = 3$), the posterior converges to the truth
(at 80 sessions: 3.03 and 3.28, HDI width about 0.5--0.6). For the right-hand
groups (true value fixed $\mu_{\beta_1} = 6$), the convergence is much slower. The HDIs remain wide, about 2 units at 40 sessions, and
reach the prior's upper bound of 8 up to 40 sessions. At 40 sessions the
right\_uni posterior mean is 6.9. The main cause is the stimulus design rather
than the prior. At a slope of 6 the rise from $\psi = 0.25$ to $\psi = 0.75$ spans
$2\log 3/6 \approx 0.37$ standardized units, less than the spacing between
stimulus levels (about 0.41), so only the two levels nearest the PSE carry
information about the slope; at the other levels the curve is already at its
floor or ceiling. The data can therefore rule out shallower slopes but not
steeper ones, and the prior's upper bound of 8 then truncates the posterior.
Replacing the bounded prior with an unbounded one removes the truncation, but not
the lack of information: that would need stimulus levels spaced more closely
around the PSE.

\paragraph{Lapse-rate means $\mu_{\gamma_h}$ and $\mu_{\gamma_l}$:} The hyperparameter
$\mu_{\gamma_h}$ is recovered, with the intervals narrowing slowly as data grows.
However, $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ is overestimated for the left-hand groups, whose true values
($-3.8$, $-3.9$) lie well below the prior center of $-2.5$: at 40 sessions
the posterior means are $-3.30$ and $-3.57$, and only at 80 sessions does left\_uni
reach $-3.69$. More trials per session do not fix this: at 320 trials
left\_uni is still at $-2.62$ (true $-3.8$). The right-hand groups show no
consistent direction of error. The HDIs contain the true value in nearly every
fit, so the error is inside the stated uncertainty. However, the posterior
means are systematically too high for the left-hand groups.

The lapse rates are confounded by the shape of the curve to a certain extent, so some identifiability issues are to be expected. The overestimation of $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ suggests a wider prior would be beneficial.

\paragraph{PSE, JND, $\beta_0$, $\beta_1$.}
Error and HDI width fall steadily as trials per session grow
(Figure~\ref{fig:session_trials}); at 320 trials the RMSE of the PSE is
0.05--0.14 and of the JND 0.01--0.03 (Table~\ref{tab:session320}). The largest
drop is between 40 and 80 trials; below 80 trials the JND in particular is
poorly determined (RMSE up to 0.5 at 20 trials). Adding sessions at a fixed 308
trials (Figure~\ref{fig:session_sessions}) also improves the session estimates,
because better-estimated hyperparameters give better shrinkage.

\paragraph{Coverage.}
The coverage of $\beta_1$, JND and both lapse rates is essentially 1.0: the HDIs are
wider than needed, a consequence of the overestimated between-session sds. PSE
coverage at 320 trials is slightly below nominal (0.89--0.94). With 84 sessions
per group, the standard error of a coverage estimate near 0.95 is about 0.024,
so 0.89 is about 2.5 standard errors low. This mild undercoverage of the PSE is
worth keeping in mind but is not large. At 40 sessions, one replicate's upward
error in $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for left\_bi (posterior mean 3.45) lowers the coverage of
$\beta_1$ and JND in that group to 0.86 and 0.88, which shows that errors at the
population level carry through to every session of the group.

\paragraph{Lapse rate $\gamma_l$ for left\_uni.}
Its RMSE stays at about 0.02 regardless of the number of trials
(Figure~\ref{fig:session_trials}). The true session values are about 0.0055;
the posterior means average 0.024 at 320 trials, roughly four times too high.
This is the $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ bias from Section~3 passed down to every session
through shrinkage. In absolute terms the error is small (a lapse rate of about
2\% instead of 0.5\%), but relative to the true value it is large.

\subsection{Conclusions for the real data}

At the size of the real dataset (42 sessions of about 308 trials):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reliable:} per-session PSE and JND; group means $\mu_{\beta_0}$;
        $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for the left-hand groups; the bias spread $\sigma_{\beta_0}$;
        the lapse-rate mean $\mu_{\gamma_h}$.
  \item \textbf{Weakly constrained:} $\mu_{\beta_1}$ for the right-hand groups, whose
        HDIs are about 2 units wide and reach the prior bound of 8.
  \item \textbf{Biased:} $\mu_{\gamma_l}$ and the per-session $\gamma_l$ for the
        left-hand groups, which are estimated too high, apparently pulled
        toward the prior center of $-2.5$.
  \item \textbf{Not identified:} $\sigma_{\beta_1}$, $\sigma_{\gamma_h}$,
        $\sigma_{\gamma_l}$ when their true values are near zero. Posteriors for
        these reflect the priors and should not be interpreted.
  \item Session-level HDIs are, if anything, conservative (coverage near or
        above 95\%), with a mild undercoverage for the PSE.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Possible follow-ups.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Done after these simulations:} the $\mu_{\beta_1}$ prior is now
        $\text{LogNormal}(\log 4,\ 0.5)$, with no upper bound. Rerunning the
        convergence study would check how the right-hand slopes are recovered
        without the truncation.
  \item Test whether the $\gamma_l$ bias comes from the $\mathcal{N}(-2.5,1)$
        prior on $\mu_\gamma$, by refitting with a wider or lower-centered prior.
        \textit{Prior changed after these simulations:} $\mu_\gamma$ is now
        $\mathcal{N}(-3,\ 1.5)$. Rerunning the convergence study will show how much
        of the bias remains; part of it is expected to come from the left\_uni
        curve not reaching its ceiling within the stimulus range.
  \item Consider tighter priors on $\sigma_{\beta_1}$ and the lapse-rate sds, which
        would shrink the session estimates more and narrow their HDIs, if
        small between-session variation is plausible for the real animals.
        \textit{Done after these simulations:} $\sigma_{\beta_1}$ now has an
        $\text{Exponential}(\lambda = 1)$ prior (mean 1, previously 4), and the
        lapse-rate sds a $\text{HalfNormal}(0.5)$ prior (previously
        $\text{HalfNormal}(1.25)$).
\end{enumerate}
